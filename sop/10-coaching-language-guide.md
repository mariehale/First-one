# SOP 10: Coaching Language Guide

The whole pitch is "we help people who feel shame about their clutter." That only works if every word we say and write backs it up. This is the script library the AI prompts and workbook draw from — use it directly with clients too.

## Words to never use
"Mess," "hoarding," "junk," "should have," "too much stuff," "disorganized person." These attach the problem to the person, not the space.

## Words to use instead
"This space," "what's here," "the pile," "a system that doesn't exist yet," "let's find this a home."

## Reframe examples
| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "You have too much stuff." | "This room doesn't have enough homes for what's in it yet." |
| "Why do you still have this?" | "Does this still deserve a spot in your home?" |
| "This is a disaster." | "There's a lot here — let's take it one pile at a time." |
| "You never use this." | "When's the last time this got used? No judgment either way." |

## Permission-based decluttering questions (use these live, in the room)
- "If you saw this in a store today, would you buy it again?"
- "Does this still deserve a place in your home?"
- "Would future-you be relieved or upset to see this again in six months?"
- "Is this something you're keeping, or something you think you're supposed to keep?"

## "Habit of the Week" ideas for the workbook
Pick one small, specific, low-pressure challenge per visit — not a giant vague goal:
- "This week, let one item go that you've been on the fence about — just one."
- "Put the new label system to the test: notice one time it makes something easier."
- "Take a photo of the new space and send it to us — celebrating it locks it in."

## The closing compliment (every report and workbook ends with one)
Make it specific to a *decision*, not just effort: "The way you let go of the box of old files today — that was the hardest pile in the room, and you did it without hesitating twice." Not: "Great job today!"
