# SOP 10: Coaching Language Guide

The whole pitch is "we help people who feel shame about their clutter — and we do it with personality, not a clipboard." That only works if every word we say and write backs it up. This is the script library the AI prompts and workbook draw from — use it directly with clients too.

**The one rule everything below follows:** *we read the clutter, never the client.* All the sass, drama, and shade is aimed at the pile of junk mail — never at the person who let it pile up. See `BRAND.md` for the full voice system (tagline hierarchy, signature concepts, when to dial personality up vs. down).

## Words to never use
"Mess," "hoarding," "junk," "should have," "too much stuff," "disorganized person." These attach the problem to the person, not the space.

## Words to use instead
"This space," "what's here," "the pile," "a system that doesn't exist yet," "let's find this a home." — and, once rapport is there, the brand's own vocabulary: "the Reveal," "werk," "Honey."

## Reframe examples
| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "You have too much stuff." | "This room doesn't have enough homes for what's in it yet." |
| "Why do you still have this?" | "Does this still deserve a spot in your home?" |
| "This is a disaster." | "There's a lot here, Honey — let's take it one pile at a time." |
| "You never use this." | "When's the last time this got used? No judgment either way." |

## Permission-based decluttering questions (use these live, in the room)
- "If you saw this in a store today, would you buy it again?"
- "Does this still deserve a place in your home?"
- "Would future-you be relieved or upset to see this again in six months?"
- "Is this something you're keeping, or something you think you're supposed to keep?"
- "Honey. Does this spark anything besides guilt?" (use with a client who's clearly ready for a little levity — read the room first)

## Reading the room: how much personality to bring
Not every client wants full swagger in the moment — some are raw, some are private, some just want it done. The brand voice can always go big in the workbook and site copy afterward; live, in the room, follow the client's lead:
- If they're laughing and matching your energy → lean in. Use "Honey," use "the Reveal," have fun with it.
- If they're quiet, tearful, or clearly protecting themselves → stay in the permission-based questions above, soft and slow. The playfulness can wait for the workbook, once the hard part's done.
- Either way, the closing compliment (below) always lands — it just changes register.

## "Habit of the Week" ideas for the workbook
Pick one small, specific, low-pressure challenge per visit — not a giant vague goal:
- "This week, let one item go that you've been on the fence about — just one. Honey, let it go."
- "Put the new label system to the test: notice one time it makes something easier."
- "Take a photo of the new space and send it to us — celebrating it locks it in."

## The closing compliment (every report and workbook ends with one)
Make it specific to a *decision*, not just effort, and let it have a little flourish: "The way you let go of that box of old files today — that was the hardest pile in the room, and you didn't hesitate twice. Honey. Iconic." Not: "Great job today!"

## The Reveal moment
When you get to the after-photo in the workbook or in person, treat it like one: a beat of anticipation, then the reveal. "Okay. Are you ready? ...Here's your pantry." This isn't extra — the drama is doing real work, because it makes the client actually *look* at what changed instead of skimming past it.
