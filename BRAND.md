# Brand Sheet

Everything in this repo (reports, workbooks, listings, emails, the GHL setup, the microsite) uses this so it all feels like one business, not scattered documents. Name and identity are confirmed — not a placeholder.

## Identity
- **Business name:** The Honey Method
- **One-liner:** A mother-daughter team that organizes your home, sells what you don't need, and reads your clutter with love until you're ready to let it go.
- **Voice (3 words):** Playful, fierce, warm.
- **The rule everything else follows:** *We read the clutter, never the client.* All the sass, drama, and shade is aimed at the pile of junk mail — never at the person who let it pile up. Same as a good drag mother reading her daughters: it's tough love with a hand extended the whole time.

## Tagline hierarchy
Different lines for different moments — pick the one that fits where the client is standing emotionally.

| Line | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **"Clutter, Meet Your Match."** | Hero line — masthead, site headline, first impression |
| **"Honey! No Shame — We've Got You."** | First touch: intake form header, waiver opening line, walking in the door |
| **"Honey, Let It Go."** | The letting-go moment — mid-session, workbook "Habit of the Week" |
| **"Honey, You Deserve More."** | Referral ask, self-worth beats, closing notes |

## Signature concepts
These aren't just taglines — they're features. Use the actual terms in client-facing material, not just internally.
- **The Reveal.** What used to be "After photos" — treat the after-shot like a runway moment. A little drama before the reveal is earned, not cheesy, because the transformation is real.
- **The Library Is Open.** Nickname for the free discovery walkthrough — ballroom slang for "time for a loving read." Sets the tone before the first visit even happens: *we're here to read your space, gently, with love.*
- **The House of Honey.** The referral family. A client who refers someone joins the House — a line in the final workbook ("Welcome to the House of Honey"), not just a discount code. See `sop/09` section 7.
- **"Let's Werk This Room."** Session-day energy — the on-site kickoff line.

## Colors (used in the workbook template and microsite)
| Role | Color | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary (headers, buttons) | Honey gold | `#D98E2B` | The confident base — everywhere |
| Spotlight (Reveal moments, big declarative lines) | Magenta spotlight | `#D6408F` | Used sparingly — The Reveal section, hero taglines, big wins. Not for body text or every button. |
| Secondary (soft accents) | Blush pink | `#F2C6C2` | Tender/vulnerable beats — coaching notes, reflection prompts |
| Background | Cream | `#FDF8F0` | Base background |
| Text | Warm charcoal | `#3A322C` | Body copy |
| Success / progress | Soft sage | `#8FA98E` | Progress bars, completion states |

The system holds two registers at once: **magenta + bold type for swagger** (celebrating a win, the Reveal, a referral ask) and **soft italic serif + blush for tenderness** (coaching prompts, the hard-to-let-go moments). Both are the same brand — don't pick one and drop the other.

## Voice rules (use everywhere — client emails, workbook, reports)
1. Never use "mess," "hoarding," "junk," or "should have." Use "space," "pile," "items," "what's here now."
2. Describe the *system*, not the person. "This closet doesn't have a home for shoes yet," not "you never put shoes away." The read is always aimed at the room, never the person in it.
3. Lead with what got done before what's left.
4. Every client-facing document ends with one specific, genuine compliment about something they did that day (a decision they made, not just "great job") — and it's allowed to be delivered with a little flourish. "The way you let that box go without blinking? Honey. Iconic."
5. Personality scales with the format: full swagger and slang (werk, reveal, House of Honey) belong in the workbook, the site, and social — places a referral-only audience opted into. Automated transactional messages (booking confirmations, reminders) stay warm and use the four taglines, but dial the insider slang back so they're instantly clear to anyone, first-time client or not.
6. See `sop/10-coaching-language-guide.md` for the full script library.
